# Product Hypotheses & Validation Plan

**Document status:** Product Discovery  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-11  
**Author:** Andrii Severyn

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the assumptions behind Cubiland's product concept and the research activities used to evaluate them.

The purpose of validation is to understand whether the identified problems are meaningful to the target audience, whether the proposed product experience addresses those problems, and which features deserve investment.

A hypothesis remains an assumption until supported by sufficient evidence.

## 2. Core Product Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Product area affected | Validation method |
|---|---|---|---|
| HYP-001 | Speedcubers find it inconvenient to combine tutorials, timers, algorithm references, and statistics across separate tools | Integrated platform proposition | User interviews and workflow observation |
| HYP-002 | Users value structured learning paths over searching for individual tutorials | Courses and lessons | Interviews and prototype testing |
| HYP-003 | Users want practice activities connected directly to the lessons they complete | Training plans and exercises | Interviews and clickable prototype |
| HYP-004 | Users value solve history and performance statistics as part of the learning experience | Timer and analytics | Interviews and prototype testing |
| HYP-005 | A connected learning and training experience can encourage users to return regularly | Engagement and retention | MVP usage analytics and cohort analysis |
| HYP-006 | Some users are willing to pay for additional learning content or advanced functionality | Free/Premium model | Pricing interviews, landing-page tests, and paid pilot |
| HYP-007 | Challenges, competitions, community features, and virtual rewards can improve engagement | Gamification and social features | Prototype testing and post-launch cohort analysis |
| HYP-008 | Users want one platform to support multiple puzzles and solving methods | Long-term product expansion | Interviews across puzzle communities |

## 3. Validation Approach

### 3.1 User Interviews

Interviews will include people from beginner, intermediate, and advanced speedcubing segments.

An initial qualitative round may target 5–8 participants per segment where access allows. This is a practical starting point, not a statistically representative sample.

For each interview, record:

- Participant segment and experience level.
- Tools currently used.
- Recent learning or training workflow.
- Main frustrations and workarounds.
- Most valuable proposed capabilities.
- Evidence of willingness to pay.
- Relevant quotes, with permission and without unnecessary personal data.

### 3.2 Competitor Evaluation

Each direct competitor will be evaluated using a consistent process:

1. Create an account where appropriate.
2. Complete or inspect a representative learning workflow.
3. Test the timer and statistics where available.
4. Review pricing and plan restrictions.
5. Review recent user feedback.
6. Record product strengths, limitations, and usability observations.
7. Record the date and source for time-sensitive findings.

### 3.3 Prototype Validation

A clickable prototype will be used to evaluate whether users can:

- Find and start an appropriate course.
- Understand a lesson.
- Move from a lesson to a related practice activity.
- Record a solve.
- Review their progress.
- Understand the distinction between Free and Premium access.

Participants should complete tasks without step-by-step guidance. Observations should focus on hesitation, misunderstandings, and points where the workflow is unclear.

### 3.4 Monetization Validation

Willingness to pay will be evaluated through current behavior and concrete choices rather than relying only on hypothetical questions.

Research will investigate:

- Which speedcubing resources users currently pay for.
- Which paid benefits they value.
- Which features they expect to remain free.
- Preferred monthly or annual billing.
- Price sensitivity.
- Reasons users would cancel a subscription.

A real purchase, deposit, or paid pilot provides stronger evidence than a positive response to a hypothetical question.

## 4. Evidence Log

Research findings will be recorded using the following structure:

| Date | Hypothesis ID | Method | Evidence | Result | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TBD | TBD | Interview / review / prototype / experiment | TBD | Supported / rejected / inconclusive | TBD |

Evidence should be specific enough to explain why a hypothesis was supported, rejected, or left inconclusive.

## 5. Decision Principles

- A hypothesis must not be considered validated based on a single positive comment.
- User statements should be distinguished from observed behavior.
- Contradictory evidence should be recorded rather than discarded.
- Product Discovery documents should be updated when evidence changes a hypothesis.
- Significant scope and monetization decisions should include their rationale.
- Assumptions without sufficient evidence should remain explicitly identified as assumptions.

## 6. Initial Validation Priorities

The first validation activities will focus on:

1. Whether fragmented learning and practice workflows are a meaningful problem.
2. Which audience segment experiences this problem most strongly.
3. Whether users value a connected learning-and-training journey.
4. Whether the timer and statistics are more useful when connected to learning progress.
5. Whether users are interested in Free and Premium learning content.
6. Whether gamification and engagement features add meaningful value after the core experience is established.

## 7. Expected Outcomes

The validation process should produce:

- A clearer definition of the primary launch audience.
- Evidence supporting or challenging the core problem statement.
- A better understanding of current user workflows and alternatives.
- A validated direction for the core learning and training experience.
- Initial evidence for the Free/Premium model.
- A prioritized set of product assumptions for the next development phase.
