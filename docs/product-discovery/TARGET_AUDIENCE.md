## 1. Product and Audience Overview

Cubiland is a gamified speedcubing learning platform designed to help users learn solving methods, practice algorithms, improve solving times, and track their development in one place.

The platform is intended for people with different levels of experience, from first-time learners to experienced speedcubers. The initial product focus is the 3×3×3 Rubik's Cube. The longer-term product direction includes additional solving methods and other twisty puzzles.

### 1.1 Target Market

| Attribute | Definition |
|---|---|
| Business model | B2C |
| Geographic scope | Worldwide |
| Intended age positioning | Broad accessibility (subject to COPPA, GDPR-K, and parental consent compliance) |
| Initial puzzle focus | 3×3×3 Rubik's Cube |
| Future product scope | Additional twisty puzzles and solving methods |
| Skill levels | Beginner, intermediate, and advanced |

The platform is designed for broad accessibility. Product availability, privacy practices, parental-consent requirements (such as COPPA and GDPR-K), community safeguards, and payment functionality will be implemented in accordance with the applicable laws and platform requirements in each target market.

### 1.2 Audience Segments

| Segment | Primary goals | Main needs |
|---|---|---|
| Beginners | Learn to solve a Rubik's Cube from the beginning | Clear explanations, structured lessons, guided practice, and visible progress |
| Improving cubers | Improve speed, consistency, and solving technique | Training plans, algorithm practice, a timer, solve history, and statistics |
| Advanced cubers | Refine techniques and practice specific cases | Advanced methods, focused drills, detailed performance analysis, and specialized content |
| Puzzle enthusiasts | Learn and practice different twisty puzzles | Multiple courses, puzzle-specific learning materials, algorithms, and progress records |

Cubiland is intended to support these segments as the platform develops. The initial release will establish the core learning and training experience around the 3×3×3 cube.

## 2. User Needs and Motivations

### 2.1 Primary User Needs

Users need to be able to:

- Learn how to solve a puzzle through an understandable learning path.
- Study solving methods and algorithms in a structured order.
- Know which lesson or skill to work on next.
- Practice newly learned concepts.
- Measure solving times and review solve history.
- Understand how their performance changes over time.
- Keep learning materials, practice activities, and performance information connected.

### 2.2 User Motivations

The main motivations addressed by Cubiland are:

- Improving personal solving times and consistency.
- Learning a new skill and progressing from beginner to advanced methods.
- Reaching personal performance goals.
- Enjoying puzzle solving and regular practice.
- Participating in challenges, competitions, and community activities.
- Receiving recognition for completed learning milestones and achievements.

## 3. Problem Analysis

### 3.1 Core Problem

People who want to learn or improve at speedcubing often use separate resources for tutorials, algorithm references, timers, training tools, notes, and statistics. When these resources are disconnected, it can be difficult to maintain a structured learning routine and connect practice activities with measurable progress.

Cubiland addresses this problem by bringing learning, algorithm practice, timed solving, and progress tracking together in one platform.

This problem statement represents the product's current discovery hypothesis and will be validated through user research.

### 3.2 Supporting Problems

| ID | Problem | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| PA-001 | Learning materials are distributed across websites, videos, and documents | Users spend time finding and organizing learning resources |
| PA-002 | Some explanations and algorithm references are difficult to follow | Users may struggle to understand or memorize solving methods |
| PA-003 | Lessons and practice activities are not always connected | Users may not know what to practice after learning a concept |
| PA-004 | Timers and performance statistics may be separate from learning progress | Users need to combine information from multiple tools |
| PA-005 | Users may not have a clear view of their current skill level or next learning goal | Training can feel unstructured and progress can be difficult to evaluate |

### 3.3 Current Alternatives

The product is being designed in the context of existing alternatives such as:

- YouTube tutorials and educational videos.
- Online speedcubing timers.
- Algorithm databases and written guides.
- Separate algorithm trainers and practice tools.
- Personal notes and manually maintained training plans.

These alternatives provide useful capabilities. Cubiland's product opportunity is to connect the relevant parts of this workflow rather than assume that existing tools are ineffective.

### 3.4 Problem Validation

The following areas will be investigated during user research:

| Research area | Question |
|---|---|
| Tool switching | How often do users move between different learning and practice tools? |
| Learning friction | Which parts of learning or training are most difficult to manage? |
| Resource discovery | How much effort is spent finding and organizing materials? |
| Segment differences | Which audience segment experiences these problems most strongly? |
| Training consistency | Do disconnected tools make it harder to practice regularly? |
| Progress visibility | How do users currently measure and understand improvement? |

## 5. Problem Statement and Product Opportunity

### 5.1 Problem Statement

Speedcubers use a combination of tutorials, algorithm references, timers, practice tools, and personal notes. Because these resources are often spread across different services, users may find it difficult to maintain a consistent learning process and connect training activities with measurable progress.

### 5.2 Product Opportunity

Cubiland will provide an integrated environment where users can learn solving methods, practice algorithms, complete training activities, record solving times, and track their development. Gamified progression and rewards will support engagement while keeping learning and skill improvement at the center of the experience.

## 6. User Research Plan

User research will focus on actual behavior and existing workflows.

Suggested interview questions:

1. How did you learn to solve your first puzzle?
2. Which websites, applications, videos, or tools do you currently use?
3. What do you use to record solving times and statistics?
4. Which parts of your current workflow are inconvenient?
5. How do you decide what to practice next?
6. How do you measure improvement?
7. Have you paid for speedcubing courses, tools, or learning materials?
8. What would make you consider switching to another platform?
9. Which parts of an integrated learning and training platform would be most useful?
10. What would make you return to a learning platform regularly?

Interviews should focus on recent experiences and real behavior rather than leading participants toward a preferred answer.
